the builders came up the hill with stone sledges and rope, and they cut the top of it flat over three generations. the elders who started the earthworks were dead before the first ring of stones went up. nobody alive at the finish remembered why the hill had to be levelled in the first place, only that it was owed.

they set the standing stones in a ring around the flat, spike side up, leaning out from the centre like they were trying to get away from something buried there. from a distance the ring looked like a crown of broken teeth. that was the idea. an army looking up at it from the valley would break formation just deciding how to get through, and a broken formation dies easy.

in the middle of the ring they planted one whitethorn, a sapling no taller than a child, and they told their children not to dig there, not ever, no matter what they found sticking up out of the ground. they told their children's children the same thing, except by then it wasn't dig there, it was don't wake it. the reason had already worn off the warning like paint off a stone.

they bred the cats for the glow generations before the hill was even chosen. by the time the ring went up the cats were already strange, already glowing faint green in the dark like something left too long in water, already known for showing up wherever the ground was bad. so when the cats started gathering thick around the base of the whitethorn, nobody needed telling twice. the cats did the remembering the people couldn't manage anymore.

it worked for longer than anyone who built it would have believed. centuries of travelers turning back at the crown of teeth, centuries of children raised on don't wake it, centuries of cats glowing soft in the grass at the treeline like a fence made of light.

what finally came through wasn't an army. it was a small clan, hungry, moving fast ahead of something worse behind them, who saw a hill ringed in stone and a solitary healthy tree growing in dead flat ground and read it the only way starving people can read anything: shelter, and wood.

they didn't hear the myth because the myth wasn't theirs. they cut the whitethorn down clean, used the trunk for the first roof beam, and built outward from there, houses stacked on houses, the old ring of teeth reduced over a few seasons to something they walked past without seeing, the way you stop seeing your own furniture.

the ground under the town had been quiet a long, long time. whatever had been sleeping in it woke up slow, the way it always had, patient and undramatic, moving through root and well water and marrow. nobody in the new town connected the first deaths to the beam holding up the roof over their heads. why would they. it was just wood.

the birds arrived before anyone thought to leave.
